The real soil mechanics under the hood

Every behavior in the toy maps to actual geotechnical physics — here's the cheat sheet.

What you seeThe real physics
Sand slumps to a slopeAngle of repose. Loose dry sand is only stable up to ~30–35°, set by its friction angle φ (grain friction + interlocking). Pile it steeper and it avalanches until stable again — exactly what the diagonal-slump rule produces.
Mud stands steeperCohesion. Clay-size particles bond electrochemically, adding cohesion c. That lets clay hold a near-vertical face (short-term) where sand can't — the reason a trench wall in clay stands and in sand collapses.
Water finds its levelHydrostatic equilibrium. Water flows from high hydraulic head to low until head is equal everywhere. Pore pressure grows with depth: u = γw·h  (γw ≈ 9.81 kN/m³).
Sand sinks in waterBuoyancy + density. Sand grains are denser than water (γsand > γw), so they settle through it — the same grain sorting that builds real sedimentary layers.

Mohr–Coulomb strength

It all reduces to one equation: soil resists shear with a friction part and a cohesion part. Sand is nearly all friction (c≈0); clay carries real cohesion — which is why they pile so differently in the box.

τf = c + σ′ · tan φ

Effective stress & liquefaction

Strength depends on grain-to-grain stress, not total stress — Terzaghi's principle. Saturate loose sand and raise pore pressure u, and σ′ → 0: the sand briefly behaves like a liquid. That's liquefaction, and it's why saturated sand here flows so easily.

σ′ = σ − u

Grain size is destiny

Gravel > sand > silt > clay, by particle size (USCS classification). Coarse soils drain fast (high permeability k) and lean on friction; fine soils drain slowly and lean on cohesion. Size sets the behavior.

gravel · sand · silt · clay

Why a cellular automaton works

Real soil is a continuum you'd normally solve with finite elements. But give each cell a few simple local rules — fall, slump, flow — and the bulk behavior emerges on its own, strikingly close to the real thing. Simple rules, complex world.

local rules → emergent mechanics
